# Design Notes

## Aligning the dashboard with business needs

The purpose of this dashboard is to help executives reduce churn, identify what is driving it, and make informed retention decisions. The contract type and age group charts highlight which customer segments are most likely to leave, so decision makers know where to focus. The customer contacts chart tests whether repeated support contact is related to churn, and the state map shows where churn is geographically concentrated, both pointing toward possible root causes such as service quality or customer experience gaps. Because the dashboard is filterable, executives can test their own hypotheses and compare segments directly, which supports building targeted retention strategies rather than one size fits all fixes.

## Presenting to a technical audience versus a nontechnical audience

There are three ways the presentation approach would change depending on the audience:

**Simplicity versus detail.** A nontechnical audience needs quick, clear insights, so the presentation stays focused on the headline KPIs and a small number of visuals. A technical audience benefits from more depth, including additional variables and more complex views, because they need a fuller picture of the analysis.

**Visuals versus statistics.** Nontechnical viewers understand patterns faster through visuals, so the dashboard leans on charts and color to tell the story. Technical viewers expect exact values and supporting calculations, so a technical version of this presentation would surface more precise statistics alongside the visuals.

**Language and terminology.** For a nontechnical audience, the language stays simple and avoids jargon so the insights are immediately clear. For a technical audience, more precise terminology is appropriate, since that audience expects it and uses it to evaluate the analysis more rigorously.

## Storytelling elements used

**A clear narrative arc, from problem to insight to action.** The KPIs establish the scale of the churn problem, and the charts explain where and why it is happening. This structure guides the viewer step by step, making it easier to connect the data directly to a business decision instead of leaving them to interpret raw numbers on their own.

**Visual emphasis on the highest value findings.** Color and placement are used deliberately, drawing attention to the KPIs and the contract type chart first. This lets a viewer identify the most important patterns quickly, without needing to study every chart in detail before understanding the main takeaway.
